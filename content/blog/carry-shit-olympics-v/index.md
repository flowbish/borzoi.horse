The Carry Shit Olympics is a underground cargo bike racing event run each year in Portland: see [their Instagram page for more details](https://www.instagram.com/csopdx). The event includes a 24-hour scavenger hunt, cargo bike polo, and a tight cargo circuit race. This is the fifth year of its existence and my first time competing! I'll be talking about my experience and sharing a bunch of photos I took at the event.

Should you attend the Carry Shit Olympics next year? **HELL YES**. If you like big bikes or heavy bikes or people that like weird bikes, or if you like carrying too much shit on your bike and always wish you had another rack to strap cargo down to, or if you like exploring Portland by bike and want to do that while carrying several pound of shit for fame and glory then this is the event for you. This was the most fun bike event I did this summer (or maybe it's tied for [the Plaid Century](/blog/plaid-century))

# My Bike

I got a cargo bike earlier this year: my shiny new Larry vs Harry E-Bullitt in "bollocks" (dump truck yellow). When my white Prius was totaled in a hit and run collision (no injuries) I decided to go back to being car-free and I have no regrets so far. The Bullitt is so much fun to ride and lets me do 95% of everything I did with my car, besides getting me over to Beaverton when the occasion arises.

# The Olympics

## The 24-hour Scavenger Hunt

The scavenger hunt started on Friday night with manifests handed out to all competitors.

{{ <gallery.embed manifest fragment="manifest" /> }}

Each task is worth some points, and each group has a minimum number of tasks needed in order to count as finished. Some of the places were faaaar, and in opposite directions, so completing everything was only theoretically possible. 

### Day 1

I spent my Friday night grabbing some pizza from Little Caesars, riding up to Kelly Point Park, and enjoying my then-warm'n'ready pizza under the full moon at midnight. 

{{ <gallery.embed manifest fragment="kelley-point-pizza" /> }}

I then wandered down into Saint Johns, dipped my bike's tire into the Willamette, thought about riding up the Crown Zellerbach trail but chickened out, then wandered up Saltzman Road off Highway 30 until I found this fun little art installation:

{{ <gallery.embed manifest fragment="seismic-piknic" /> }}

And then back home for a few hours of sleep.

### Day 2

In the morning I biked down to do the physical challenge, which was just doing a few laps around the Ladd's rose gardens, and then I shot out to Powell Butte for the peacefull Springwater trail ride and nice scenic nature. While I was up at the mountain finder, my headphones slipped out of my pocket, which I managed to recover on [my epic headphones side quest](#headphones-side-quest).

{{ <gallery.embed manifest fragment="faceless-statue" />}}

I did a few smaller tasks, found some statues, worked my way up to Council Crest park, and crossed the last few bridges I needed before heading back toward the finish. I knew I was close on the hundred mile challenge though, so I made sure to spend my last half hour getting in some laps around Ladd's Addition as a little insurance.

## Cargo Polo

New up was cargo bike polo, a novel game requiring two players per bike, closer to the polo roots than many of its other spin-offs. I didn't end up competing in this one, but I got to get in some photography practice, and got a lot of experience with my new film camera!

## Headphones Side Quest

After cargo polo, I decided to ride out to Gresham to recover my lost headphones. They have the Android Find My Device feature which gives me very limited GPS tracking when other people's phones are nearby, but it was giving me an ~5 block radius that was shifting around, so my plan was to head out to where I thought the center of that area was and wander around until my phone actually made contact with them. This turned out to be a lot slower than I expected, because five blocks is quite a large area and the positioning kept shifting around in a way that made me second-guess if I was even in the right area.

{{ <gallery.embed manifest fragment="headphones-hunt" /> }}

After an hour of roaming Gresham, the circle finally narrowed down to the corner of one block, and when I went over there my phone locked on to a single apartment building. I roamed around it a bit until I was pretty sure I was right next to the unit, and pressed the button that make the headphones play an annoying chirp. I heard it come out through the apartment window! I called up to the window, and a man replied and handed down the headphones through a crack in the window screen (I handed up some cash as thanks).

## Cargo Circuit Race

The final event was a cargo bike circuit race. Each race involved a lap on a tight circuit course, loading up your bike with some cargo, and then racing three more laps. 

From watching the races, the front-loader cargo bikes had a huge disadvantage on the turns, but made up for it by being extremely quick to load cargo in. THe more bike-shaped bikes took significantly longer to load up, to the point it seemed they couldn't make it up. Also, with how tight the turns were it seemed impossible to pass a front-loader sometimes.

{{ <gallery.embed manifest fragment="zack-raw-bullitt" /> }}

I raced in the e-bike category and ended up getting third place! I don't think this was particularly impressive, as there were only five racers total in the division, but it was fun to get something. I also ended up winning third place overall for the weekend, based on vibes and probably for showing up to all of the events.
